Expressions and substitution

By the end of this lesson you can:
  • use letters to stand for numbers and write expressions from words
  • collect like terms to simplify an expression
  • substitute numbers (including negatives) into expressions and formulae

1. Letters stand for numbers

A letter (a variable) stands for a number we don't know yet, or one that can change.

3n means 3 × n. n² means n × n. n/4 means n ÷ 4. We leave out the × sign.

An expression is made of terms joined by + and −. In 5x + 3y − 7 the terms are 5x, 3y and −7.

2. Collecting like terms

Like terms have exactly the same letters and powers, so they can be added or subtracted. 3x and 5x are like terms. 3x and 3x² are not.

Predict first: Is 3x + 2x equal to 5x or 5x²?
Check my prediction

5x. Think of it as 3 x's and 2 more x's, which makes 5 x's. The power doesn't change.

Type an expression, or tap an example.

3. Substituting numbers

To substitute, replace each letter with its number. Put brackets round negative numbers, and remember the order of operations: x² with x = −2 is (−2)² = 4.
Predict first: If x = −2, is 3x² equal to 12 or −12?
Check my prediction

12. Square first: (−2)² = 4, then multiply: 3 × 4 = 12. The power only applies to the x, which is wrapped in its own brackets once you substitute.
