The index laws

By the end of this lesson you can:
  • multiply and divide powers of the same letter by adding and subtracting indices
  • find a power of a power by multiplying indices
  • simplify expressions with coefficients such as 3x⁴ × 5x and 10x³ ÷ 2x²

1. Multiplying and dividing powers

x³ means x × x × x, and x² means x × x. So x³ × x² = x × x × x × x × x = x⁵.

Multiply: add the indices. xᵐ × xⁿ = xᵐ⁺ⁿ

Divide: subtract the indices. xᵐ ÷ xⁿ = xᵐ⁻ⁿ

Power of a power: multiply the indices. (xᵐ)ⁿ = xᵐⁿ

The letter on its own, x, has index 1. The rules only work when the base (the letter) is the same.

Predict first: Is x³ × x² equal to x⁵ or x⁶?
Check my prediction

x⁵. Add the indices: 3 + 2 = 5. (Don't multiply them: that is the rule for a power of a power.)

Type a product, a quotient or a power. Use ^ for powers, × and ÷ (or *, /).

2. Numbers in front (coefficients)

Deal with the numbers and the letters separately. 3x⁴ × 5x: numbers 3 × 5 = 15; letters x⁴ × x¹ = x⁵. So the answer is 15x⁵. For 10x³ ÷ 2x²: 10 ÷ 2 = 5 and x³ ÷ x² = x, so 5x.

3. Powers of brackets

With (2x³)² the power outside applies to everything inside: 2² × (x³)² = 4x⁶. Don't forget to square the number.
