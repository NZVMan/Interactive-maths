Zero and negative indices

By the end of this lesson you can:
  • explain why any number to the power 0 equals 1
  • rewrite a negative index as a fraction with a positive index
  • evaluate powers such as 2⁻³ and (2/3)⁻² and simplify expressions like 3x⁻²

1. The zero index

Look at the pattern as the index goes down by one. Each step divides by 2.

Predict first: What is 2⁰: 0, 1 or 2?
Check my prediction

1. Following the pattern, 2² = 4, 2¹ = 2 (divide by 2), so 2⁰ = 2 ÷ 2 = 1. In fact any number (not 0) to the power 0 is 1.

a⁰ = 1 for any a that isn't 0.

a⁻ⁿ = 1/aⁿ. A negative index means "one over" the positive power. It does not make the answer negative.

The negative index only affects the thing directly in front of it: 3x⁻² = 3/x² (the 3 stays on top).

2. Negative indices

Predict first: Is 2⁻³ equal to −8, −6 or 1/8?
Check my prediction

1/8. 2⁻³ = 1/2³ = 1/8. The negative sign in the index means "reciprocal", not a negative answer.

Type a power like 2^-3, (2/3)^-2, 5x^-1 or x^-2.

3. Fractions to a negative power

(2/3)⁻² = (3/2)²= 9/4. A negative index on a fraction flips the fraction, then you use the positive index.
