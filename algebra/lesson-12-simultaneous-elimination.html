Topic 2 · Lesson 12

Simultaneous equations: elimination

By the end of this lesson you can:
  • explain what simultaneous equations are and what their solution means
  • solve simultaneous equations by adding or subtracting to eliminate a letter
  • multiply one or both equations first when no letter matches
  • check a solution in both equations

1. Two equations, two unknowns

Simultaneous equations are two equations that are both true at the same time, like 2x + y = 7 and x − y = 2. The solution is a pair of values (x, y) that works in both.

Elimination: make the numbers in front of one letter the same size, then add or subtract the equations so that letter disappears.

If the signs of the matching terms are different (+y and −y), add. If they are the same (+y and +y), subtract.

Then solve for the remaining letter, put it back into an equation to find the other, and check in both.

Predict first: For 2x + y = 7 and x − y = 2, will adding the two equations remove x or y?
Check my prediction

y. The y terms are +y and −y, which cancel when you add. That leaves 3x = 9, so x = 3, and then y = 1.

Type two equations in x and y, or tap an example.

2. Multiplying first

For 3x + 2y = 12 and x + y = 5, no letter matches. Multiply the second equation by 2 to get 2x + 2y = 10. Now the y terms match, so subtract.

3. Multiplying both equations

Sometimes you must multiply both equations. For 2x + 3y = 13 and 3x − 2y = 0, multiply the first by 2 and the second by 3 to make the y terms 6y and −6y.
