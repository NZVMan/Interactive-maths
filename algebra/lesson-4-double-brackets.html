Expanding double brackets

By the end of this lesson you can:
  • expand two brackets by multiplying every term in the first by every term in the second
  • collect like terms in the answer, taking care with negative signs
  • expand a squared bracket such as (x + 3)²

1. Two brackets

Multiply every term in the first bracket by every term in the second. For two terms each that gives 4 products, then you collect like terms.

A grid helps: (x + 4)(x − 7) gives x × x, x × (−7), 4 × x and 4 × (−7).

Predict first: Is (x + 3)(x + 2) equal to x² + 6 or x² + 5x + 6?
Check my prediction

x² + 5x + 6. The four products are x², 2x, 3x and 6. The two x terms are like terms: 2x + 3x = 5x.

Type two brackets, or tap an example.

2. Bigger numbers in the brackets

With (2x + 3)(x − 5) multiply carefully: 2x × x = 2x², 2x × (−5) = −10x, 3 × x = 3x, 3 × (−5) = −15. Then −10x + 3x = −7x.

3. Squares of brackets

(x + 3)² means (x + 3)(x + 3). It is not x² + 9. Write it out as two brackets and expand.
