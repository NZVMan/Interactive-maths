Topic 2 · Lesson 13

Simultaneous equations: substitution, graphs and problems

By the end of this lesson you can:
  • solve simultaneous equations by substitution
  • see the solution as where two straight lines cross
  • turn a worded problem into two equations and solve it

1. Substitution

When one equation already says y = …, put that expression into the other equation. For y = 2x − 1 and 3x + y = 9, replace y in the second equation: 3x + (2x − 1) = 9, so 5x = 10 and x = 2.

Each equation is a straight line. The solution (x, y) is the point where the two lines cross, because that point is on both lines.

Predict first: The lines x + y = 6 and x − y = 2 cross at one point. Will x be bigger or smaller than y there?
Check my prediction

Bigger. x − y = 2 means x is 2 more than y. Adding the equations gives 2x = 8, so x = 4 and y = 2.

2. Worded problems

Choose letters for the two unknowns, write two equations from the story, then solve. Always check the answer makes sense in the story.
