Brackets and common factors

By the end of this lesson you can:
  • expand a bracket by multiplying every term inside by the number or term outside
  • expand and simplify more than one bracket, including with negative numbers
  • take out the highest common factor to factorise an expression

1. Expanding brackets

To expand a bracket, multiply every term inside by what is outside: 3(x + 4) = 3x + 12.

Take care with signs: a negative outside changes the sign of every term inside. −2(x − 3) = −2x + 6.

Multiply the numbers, then the letters: 3x × 2x = 6x² and 3x × 4y = 12xy.

Predict first: Is −2(x − 3) equal to −2x − 6 or −2x + 6?
Check my prediction

−2x + 6. Multiply each term: −2 × x = −2x, and −2 × (−3) = +6. A negative times a negative is positive.

Type a bracket to expand, or tap an example.

2. Taking out common factors

Factorising is expanding backwards. Find the highest common factor (HCF) of all the terms, write it outside a bracket, and divide each term by it.

Check by expanding: the answer must give you back the starting expression. Take out as much as you can: 6x + 12 = 6(x + 2), not 3(2x + 4).

Predict first: What is the HCF of 9x² and 15xy: 3, 3x or 3xy?
Check my prediction

3x. The HCF of 9 and 15 is 3. Both terms contain x (but only one has y, and only one has x²). So the HCF is 3x.

Type an expression with two or three terms.
