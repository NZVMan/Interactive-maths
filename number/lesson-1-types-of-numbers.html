Topic 1 · Lesson 1

Types of numbers, factors and multiples

By the end of this lesson you can:
  • name the number families: natural, integer, rational, irrational and real
  • spot prime, square and cube numbers
  • write a number as a product of prime factors
  • find the HCF and LCM of two numbers

1. The number families

Every number you will meet in this course is a real number. Real numbers split into families that sit inside each other:

  • Natural numbers are the counting numbers: 1, 2, 3, …
  • Integers are whole numbers, including zero and negatives: …, −2, −1, 0, 1, 2, …
  • Rational numbers can be written as a fraction of two integers, like ¾, 0.5 or −7. Their decimals either stop or repeat.
  • Irrational numbers cannot be written as a fraction. Their decimals go on forever with no repeating pattern, like π and √2.
Every natural number is an integer, every integer is rational, and every rational or irrational number is real. A number is never both rational and irrational.

Type a number and see which families it belongs to. You can use a whole number, a decimal, a fraction like 3/4, √2 or π.

2. Primes, squares and cubes

  • A prime number has exactly two factors: 1 and itself. The first few are 2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13. Note that 1 is not prime, and 2 is the only even prime.
  • A square number is a whole number times itself: 1, 4, 9, 16, 25, …
  • A cube number is a whole number times itself twice: 1, 8, 27, 64, 125, …

Use the classifier above on 49, 64 and 91. Does 91 surprise you?

3. Prime factors

Every whole number above 1 can be built from primes multiplied together, in exactly one way. A factor tree finds them: keep splitting until every branch ends in a prime (the highlighted ones).

Write 180 as a product of prime factors.

180 = 2 × 90 = 2 × 2 × 45 = 2 × 2 × 3 × 15 = 2 × 2 × 3 × 3 × 5.

In index form: 180 = 2² × 3² × 5.

In the practice box, type powers with a caret, like 2^2 x 3^2 x 5.

4. HCF and LCM

  • The highest common factor (HCF) is the biggest number that divides into both.
  • The lowest common multiple (LCM) is the smallest number that both divide into.

Write both numbers as products of primes. The primes they share go in the middle of the diagram.

HCF = multiply the numbers in the middle.
LCM = multiply everything in the diagram.
