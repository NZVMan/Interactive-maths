Directed numbers, squares and roots

By the end of this lesson you can:
  • add, subtract, multiply and divide positive and negative numbers (directed numbers)
  • use directed numbers in real situations, like temperatures and flood levels
  • work out squares, cubes, square roots, cube roots and other roots
  • calculate with them, like 3² × ⁴√16

1. Adding and subtracting directed numbers

A directed number has a size and a direction: positive (+) or negative (−). On a number line, adding moves you right and subtracting moves you left. A negative number flips the direction.

Adding a negative is the same as subtracting: 5 + (−2) = 5 − 2 = 3
Subtracting a negative is the same as adding: 5 − (−2) = 5 + 2 = 7
Predict first: What is 3 − (−4)? Will you move left or right?
Check my prediction

7. Subtracting a negative is the same as adding the positive, so you move 4 to the right.

Work out −3 − (−7).

Subtracting −7 is the same as adding 7.

−3 + 7 = 4

2. Multiplying and dividing

Same signs give a positive answer. Different signs give a negative answer.
This works for both × and ÷.
Predict first: Is (−6) × (−4) positive or negative?
Check my prediction

Positive: both numbers are negative, so the signs are the same. The answer is 24.

3. Directed numbers in real life

Directed numbers describe things that can be above or below a starting level.

  • Temperature: −4°C is four degrees below freezing.
  • Flood levels: a river level of −35 cm means 35 cm below its normal level, and +20 cm means 20 cm above it.

A rise means you add. A fall means you subtract. To find how much something changed, subtract the smaller from the larger.

A river is 25 cm below its normal level. After rain it rises by 40 cm. What is its level compared with normal?

−25 + 40 = 15

It is 15 cm above normal.

4. Squares and cubes

  • The square of a number is the number times itself: 5² = 5 × 5 = 25.
  • The cube of a number is the number times itself twice: 4³ = 4 × 4 × 4 = 64.

5. Roots

A root undoes a power.

  • The square root √ asks: what number times itself makes this? √36 = 6 because 6² = 36.
  • The cube root ∛ asks: what number, used three times, makes this? ∛125 = 5 because 5³ = 125.
  • The fourth root ⁴√ works the same way with four copies: ⁴√16 = 2 because 2⁴ = 16.
Predict first: Is √50 a whole number? If not, between which two whole numbers is it?
Check my prediction

Not a whole number. 7² = 49 and 8² = 64, so √50 is between 7 and 8 (just above 7).

6. Putting it together

Work out each power or root first, then do the rest of the calculation.

Work out 3² × ⁴√16.

3² = 9

⁴√16 = 2

9 × 2 = 18
