Indices and standard form

By the end of this lesson you can:
  • use the index laws
  • work out zero, negative and fractional indices
  • write numbers in standard form and calculate with them

1. The index laws

In an, a is the base and n is the index (or power). It says how many copies of a to multiply.

LawExample
am × an = am+n2³ × 2⁴ = 2⁷
am ÷ an = am−n5⁶ ÷ 5² = 5⁴
(am)n = amn(3²)⁴ = 3⁸

2. Zero, negative and fractional indices

a⁰ = 1 (for any a that isn't 0)
a−n = 1 ÷ an (a negative index means reciprocal, not a negative answer)
a1/n is the nth root of a, so a1/2 = √a and a1/3 = ∛a
am/n = (ⁿ√a)m (root first, then power)

3. Standard form

Standard form writes very large or very small numbers as A × 10n, where 1 ≤ A < 10 and n is an integer.

  • Big numbers have a positive n: 45 000 = 4.5 × 10⁴.
  • Small numbers have a negative n: 0.0032 = 3.2 × 10⁻³.
Predict first: Will 0.00032 have a positive or a negative index in standard form?
Check my prediction

Negative. It is a small number, so you move the decimal point to the right: 3.2 × 10⁻⁴.

Type an ordinary number to convert it.
