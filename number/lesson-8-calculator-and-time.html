Topic 1 · Lesson 8

Calculator use and time

By the end of this lesson you can:
  • use a calculator efficiently, with brackets, and round only at the end
  • check an answer for sense using an estimate
  • change between 12-hour and 24-hour time
  • find durations and read clocks, dials and timetables

1. Using a calculator efficiently

A calculator follows the order of operations. It does exactly what you type, so:

  • Put brackets round the top and the bottom of a fraction: (5.2 + 3.8) ÷ 3.
  • Use brackets for negative numbers that are squared: (−3)² = 9 but −3² = −9.
  • Don't round in the middle. Keep the full number on the screen (or use the answer key) and round only at the end.
Predict first: You want (5.2 + 3.8) ÷ 3 but type 5.2 + 3.8 ÷ 3. Will you get 3?
Check my prediction

No. Without brackets the calculator divides 3.8 by 3 first and then adds 5.2, which gives about 6.47. The correct answer is 9 ÷ 3 = 3.

Type what you meant and what you typed, or tap an example.

2. Checking your answer

Always ask: is this about the right size? Round each number to 1 significant figure (Lesson 6) and do a quick estimate. If your calculator answer is very different, check what you typed. You can also check with the inverse operation: if 48.7 × 9.8 = 477.26, then 477.26 ÷ 9.8 should give 48.7.

3. 12-hour and 24-hour time

In 24-hour time the day runs from 00:00 to 23:59, with four digits.

For times after 1 pm, add 12 hours: 3:25 pm = 15:25. Midnight is 00:00 and 12 noon is 12:00. 12:30 am is 00:30.

Predict first: What is 3:25 pm in 24-hour time: 13:25 or 15:25?
Check my prediction

15:25. 3 + 12 = 15, and the minutes stay the same.

Slide to change the time. The clock, the 12-hour time and the 24-hour time all match.

4. Durations and timetables

To find how long something takes, count on to the next whole hour, then add the rest. From 07:45 to 10:20: 15 min to 08:00, 2 h to 10:00, then 20 min. Total 2 h 35 min. Remember 1 hour = 60 minutes, not 100.
Predict first: How long is it from 10:45 to 12:15?
Check my prediction

1 h 30 min. 15 min to 11:00, 1 h to 12:00, then 15 min more, so 15 + 60 + 15 = 90 minutes.

5. Reading clocks and dials

On an analogue clock the short hand shows the hour and the long hand shows the minutes. Each big number is 5 minutes. On a dial, work out what one small division is worth first: count the small steps between two labelled numbers.
