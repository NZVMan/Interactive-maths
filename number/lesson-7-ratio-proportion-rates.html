Topic 1 · Lesson 7

Ratio, proportion and rates

By the end of this lesson you can:
  • simplify a ratio and share an amount in a given ratio
  • solve direct and inverse proportion problems
  • calculate speed, distance, time and average speed
  • use other rates (density, pressure) when the formula is given
  • use ratio and scales on maps and models

1. Ratio

A ratio compares quantities in the same units. Simplify it by dividing every part by the same number, just like a fraction: 24 : 36 = 2 : 3.

To share in a ratio: add the parts, find one part (total ÷ parts), then multiply.

Predict first: $60 is shared in the ratio 2 : 3. Does the bigger share get $30 or $36?
Check my prediction

$36. There are 2 + 3 = 5 parts, so one part is $60 ÷ 5 = $12. The shares are 2 × 12 = $24 and 3 × 12 = $36.

2. Direct and inverse proportion

  • Direct proportion: when one quantity doubles, so does the other (more pens cost more money). Find the value of one, then scale up.
  • Inverse proportion: when one quantity doubles, the other halves (more workers take fewer days). The product stays the same.
Predict first: 4 workers build a wall in 6 days. With 3 workers, will it take more than 6 days or fewer?
Check my prediction

More: 8 days. Fewer workers means more days. 4 × 6 = 24 worker-days of work, and 24 ÷ 3 = 8.

3. Speed and other rates

speed = distance ÷ time, distance = speed × time, time = distance ÷ speed.

Average speed = total distance ÷ total time. It is not the average of the speeds.

Change minutes to hours first: 30 min = 0.5 h, 45 min = 0.75 h.

Predict first: You drive 60 km at 30 km/h, then 60 km at 60 km/h. Is your average speed 45 km/h?
Check my prediction

No, it is 40 km/h. The total distance is 120 km. The times are 2 h and 1 h, so 3 h in total. 120 ÷ 3 = 40.

Fill in two boxes and leave the one you want to find blank. Distance in km, time in hours, speed in km/h.

Other rates work the same way. When a question needs a formula, it will be given, for example density = mass ÷ volume and pressure = force ÷ area.

4. Scales

A map scale of 1 : 50 000 means 1 cm on the map is 50 000 cm in real life. Multiply by the scale, then change units: ÷ 100 for metres, ÷ 100 000 for kilometres.
Predict first: On a 1 : 50 000 map, 4 cm shows a track. Is it 200 m, 2 km or 20 km long?
Check my prediction

2 km. 4 × 50 000 = 200 000 cm, and 200 000 ÷ 100 000 = 2 km.
