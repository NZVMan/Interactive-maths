Topic 1 · Lesson 5

Four rules, fractions and order of operations

By the end of this lesson you can:
  • do calculations in the right order, using brackets and powers
  • add, subtract, multiply and divide fractions, including mixed numbers and improper fractions
  • add, subtract, multiply and divide decimals
  • do all of this with positive and negative numbers

1. Order of operations

A calculation has to be done in the right order, or two people get two different answers. Work through it like this:

  1. Brackets
  2. Indices (powers and roots)
  3. Division and Multiplication, from left to right
  4. Addition and Subtraction, from left to right
Multiplication and division have equal priority, so work from the left: 8 ÷ 2 × 4 = 4 × 4 = 16. Addition and subtraction work the same way.
Predict first: Is 2 + 3 × 4 equal to 20 or 14?
Check my prediction

14. Multiplication comes before addition, so 3 × 4 = 12 first, then 2 + 12 = 14.

Type an expression, or tap an example. Use + − × ÷ ^ and brackets.

Work out 20 − 3 × (4 + 1).

Brackets: 4 + 1 = 5, so 20 − 3 × 5

Multiply: 3 × 5 = 15, so 20 − 15

Subtract: 20 − 15 = 5

2. Fractions

  • Simplify by dividing the top and bottom by their HCF: 6/8 = 3/4.
  • Add or subtract by first giving both fractions the same bottom number (a common denominator), then adding or subtracting the tops.
  • Multiply the tops together and the bottoms together.
  • Divide by flipping the second fraction and multiplying: a ÷ b = a × (flipped b).
  • A mixed number like 2 1/3 is a whole number and a fraction. Change it to an improper fraction (7/3) before you multiply or divide.
Predict first: Is 1/2 + 1/3 equal to 2/5?
Check my prediction

No. You can't add the tops and the bottoms. Use a common denominator of 6: 3/6 + 2/6 = 5/6.

Type fractions like 3/4, mixed numbers like 2 1/3, or negatives like −1/2.

3. Decimals

  • Add and subtract by lining up the decimal points.
  • Multiply by ignoring the decimal points, multiplying, then putting the point back so the answer has as many decimal places as both numbers together.
  • Divide by a decimal by multiplying both numbers by 10 (or 100) until you are dividing by a whole number: 5.6 ÷ 0.7 = 56 ÷ 7 = 8.
Predict first: Is 0.3 × 0.4 equal to 1.2 or 0.12?
Check my prediction

0.12. 3 × 4 = 12, and there are 2 decimal places altogether, so the answer is 0.12.
