Topic 1 · Lesson 9

Money and personal finance

By the end of this lesson you can:
  • convert between currencies using a given exchange rate
  • work out discounts, GST, profit and loss
  • calculate simple interest and compound interest
  • read tables of hours and pay to work out earnings

1. Changing currency

An exchange rate such as 1 NZD = 0.60 USD tells you what $1 NZ is worth. Going from NZD to another currency: multiply. Coming back to NZD: divide.
Predict first: If 1 NZD = 0.60 USD, will $100 NZ buy more than 100 US dollars or fewer?
Check my prediction

Fewer: 100 × 0.60 = 60 USD. Each NZ dollar is worth less than one US dollar.

2. Discount, GST, profit and loss

  • Discount: a price reduced by a percentage. Sale price = price × (1 − discount ÷ 100). For 20% off, multiply by 0.8.
  • GST in NZ is 15%. To add GST, multiply by 1.15.
  • Profit = selling price − cost price. Loss is when selling is less than cost.
  • Percentage profit = profit ÷ cost price × 100.
Predict first: A $80 jacket is 25% off. Is the sale price $55, $60 or $65?
Check my prediction

$60. 25% of 80 is 20, and 80 − 20 = 60. (Or multiply by 0.75.)

3. Simple and compound interest

Simple interest is the same every year: I = P × R × T ÷ 100 (P = amount invested, R = rate per year in %, T = years).

Compound interest adds the interest to the amount each year, so next year's interest is bigger. Use A = P(1 + R/100)n, where A is the final amount and n is the number of years.

Predict first: $1000 is invested at 5% for 2 years. Which gives more: simple or compound interest?
Check my prediction

Compound. Simple: 1000 × 5 × 2 ÷ 100 = $100. Compound: year 1 earns $50, but year 2 earns 5% of $1050 = $52.50, so $102.50 in total.

4. Earnings from a table

Read the table carefully. Work out the hours at the normal rate and the hours at the overtime rate separately, then add the two pay amounts.
