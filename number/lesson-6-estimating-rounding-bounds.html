Topic 1 · Lesson 6

Estimating, rounding and bounds

By the end of this lesson you can:
  • round to a number of decimal places or significant figures
  • estimate an answer by rounding each number to 1 significant figure
  • round an answer sensibly for its context (money, buses, tiles)
  • give the lower and upper bounds of a number that has been rounded

1. Rounding to decimal places and significant figures

Decimal places (d.p.) are counted after the decimal point.

Significant figures (s.f.) are counted from the first digit that is not zero.

To round, find the last digit you keep, then look at the next digit. If it is 5 or more, round up. If it is 4 or less, leave the last digit alone.

Predict first: What is 0.0764 to 2 significant figures? Is it 0.07 or 0.076?
Check my prediction

0.076. The zeros at the start don't count. The first significant figure is the 7, so you keep 7 and 6, and the next digit (4) says leave it.

Round 5.996 to 2 decimal places.

The 2nd decimal place is 9. The next digit is 6, so round the 9 up.

9 + 1 = 10, so carry: 5.99 + 0.01 = 6.00

Answer: 6.00

2. Estimating

An estimate is a quick check that an answer is about the right size. Round every number to 1 significant figure, then do the calculation with the easy numbers.

Predict first: Estimate 38.2 × 9.7. Will it be about 40, 400 or 4000?
Check my prediction

About 400. 38.2 rounds to 40 and 9.7 rounds to 10, and 40 × 10 = 400.

Type a calculation with + − × ÷ and brackets, or tap an example.

3. Rounding in context

Read what the question is about. Money is rounded to the nearest cent (2 d.p.). If you need whole things (buses, packs, tins), round up, because rounding down would leave people or tiles short.

4. Upper and lower bounds

If a length is 8 cm to the nearest cm, the real length could be anything that rounds to 8. The smallest it can be is the lower bound, and the value it can never quite reach is the upper bound.

Go half a unit either way. For 8 cm to the nearest cm: 7.5 ≤ length < 8.5. The upper bound uses < (not ≤) because 8.5 itself would round up to 9.
Predict first: A mass is 12.4 kg to 1 decimal place. What is the lower bound: 12.3, 12.35 or 12.399?
Check my prediction

12.35. The accuracy is 0.1, so go half of that (0.05) below 12.4. 12.35 rounds up to 12.4.
